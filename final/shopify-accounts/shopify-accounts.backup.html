THE ANATOMY OF A SHOPIFY PAYOUT

What is actually inside a
Shopify payout

A Shopify payout that lands in your bank is never simply sales. A single payout bundles several things together at once, and booked as one lump figure, every one of them distorts your VAT and your profit.

We reconcile each payout back to the underlying orders, usually with a specialist Shopify integration such as A2X or Link My Books feeding Xero or QuickBooks, so your accounts reflect what you actually earned, not just what hit the bank.

Fees buried in payouts

The Issue

Shopify Payments and gateway fees come out before the money lands, so your bank deposit never matches your sales.

The settlement delay

The Issue

Payouts arrive on a delay and span several order dates, so sales and cash never line up without proper reconciliation.

How We Fix It

We reconcile each payout back to the orders it covers, with A2X or Link My Books feeding Xero or QuickBooks, so cash and sales finally agree.

Refunds and chargebacks

The Issue

Netted off future payouts rather than shown cleanly, they quietly erode the margin you think you are making.

How We Fix It

We record refunds and chargebacks against the original sale, so revenue, VAT and margin stay accurate rather than overstated.

Multi-currency and FX

The Issue

Overseas sales and fees convert at different rates. Recorded wrong, they distort both your VAT position and your profit.

How We Fix It

We convert foreign-currency sales and fees consistently and book the FX correctly, so your numbers hold up across every market.

VAT across borders

The Issue

UK VAT plus EU and cross-border rules. Getting place of supply wrong is the single most expensive Shopify mistake.

How We Fix It

We set place of supply, OSS and IOSS, import VAT and registrations correctly, and document the treatment for HMRC, so you never overpay or get caught out.

Ad spend versus real margin

The Issue

Meta, Google and TikTok spend is rarely mapped to true contribution, so growth can quietly be unprofitable.

How We Fix It

We link advertising spend to margin by channel and product, so you scale what genuinely makes money rather than what only looks busy.

QUESTIONS WE ARE ASKED EVERY DAY

E-commerce accounting,
Answered plainly

What does a Shopify accountant do? +
A specialist Shopify accountant reconciles gross sales, gateway fees, refunds, and chargebacks from your settlement payouts back to individual orders. We ensure cross-border and UK VAT are calculated properly, connect inventory and sales feeds into Xero or QuickBooks, and produce management accounts that show true profit rather than just bank deposits.
Do I need to register for VAT as a Shopify seller? +
In the UK, you must register for VAT once your rolling 12-month taxable turnover passes £90,000 (or if you expect it to within 30 days). If you sell to EU consumers, you may also need to utilize the One-Stop Shop (OSS) or Import One-Stop Shop (IOSS) schemes. We track your threshold in real-time and handle all UK and cross-border registrations.
Do I need a Shopify accountant if I already use Xero or QuickBooks? +
Yes. Xero and QuickBooks are bookkeeping platforms, but raw Shopify payouts hit the bank as net lump sums. Without specialist middleware (like A2X or Link My Books) and e-commerce tax mapping, your accounts will misstate revenue, overpay or underpay VAT, and fail to account for merchant gateway fees.
Which accounting software is best for a Shopify store? +
We recommend Xero or QuickBooks Online paired with e-commerce settlement bridge software like A2X or Link My Books. This stack automatically splits fees, refunds, sales tax, and payouts into accurate daily or payout-based journal entries.
How do you record Shopify payouts correctly? +
Instead of coding the net bank deposit as a single sales line, we map gross sales, shipping collected, discounts, refunds, and gateway processing fees to their respective chart of accounts. The net settlement matches the exact deposit in your bank feed perfectly to the penny.
